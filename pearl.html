You're already in the room. You could be supplying more of it.

The sink is going in either way. So is the faucet, and usually a mirror. If you're not supplying them, someone else is billing for them on a job you're already standing in.

Adding a category usually costs more than it earns. New supplier, new terms, new minimums, another rep to chase. Most shops decide it isn't worth the overhead, and they're often right.

Clients ask for options you don't carry. When the answer is "we don't do that," the conversation moves somewhere else, and it can take the rest of the project with it.

Bigger jobs want one point of contact. Builders and designers would rather hand the whole kitchen and bath package to a shop that can cover it than coordinate four suppliers themselves.

"Our purpose is to create spaces that encourage genuine human connection."

Connection doesn't need to be forced or formal. It happens when people feel comfortable, supported, and present in the spaces around them. A kitchen sink is where a family lands at the end of the day. That's the room you're building.
